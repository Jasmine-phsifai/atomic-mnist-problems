\problemstart{AP-02-009}{A Random Direction Checks the Whole Gradient}{Difficulty 4/5}{Chapter 2 \textbullet\ numpy}{Compare $\nabla f(x)^\top d$ with a scalar directional finite difference and report a scale-aware discrepancy.}

        \subsection{Mathematical statement}


For nonzero direction $\bm d$, normalize $\hat{\bm d}=\bm d/\|\bm d\|_2$ and
compare
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=\nabla f(\bm x)^\top\hat{\bm d},\qquad
n=\frac{f(\bm x+h\hat{\bm d})-f(\bm x-h\hat{\bm d})}{2h},\\
E_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{|a-n|}{\max(1,|a|,|n|)}.
\end{gathered}
\]
One direction compresses a full gradient into one scalar probe; several seeded
directions provide a distribution of evidence.

        \subsection{Theory--engineering gap inventory}

        \begin{gapbox}
        \textbf{Explicit gap.} Coordinate equality is expensive in high dimension, while a projection trades localization for broad coverage and introduces randomness/normalization choices.

        \textbf{Implicit gap exposed by the result.} An intentionally corrupted gradient produces a shifted error distribution across random directions even when an unluckily chosen coordinate could miss the defect.
        \end{gapbox}

        \subsection{Acceptance evidence}

        \begin{evidencebox}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item A quadratic function produces a small scale-aware error and leaves all inputs unchanged.
  \item Zero direction, shape mismatch, nonfinite values, and invalid step fail with \code{ValueError}.
  \item The benchmark reports median/max errors for correct and corrupted gradients over the same seeded directions. (Trust-based: the test suite does not machine-check benchmark output.)
        \end{itemize}
        \end{evidencebox}
