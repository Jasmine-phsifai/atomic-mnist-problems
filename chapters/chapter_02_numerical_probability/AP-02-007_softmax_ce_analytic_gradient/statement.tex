        \subsection{Mathematical statement}


For mean sparse cross-entropy
\[
  L(Z,y)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \left(\log\sum_{k=1}^{K}e^{Z_{ik}}-Z_{i,y_i}\right),
\]
derive the Jacobian contribution of each row, then implement
\[
  G=\nabla_ZL\in\mathbb R^{N\times K}.
\]
Your derivation should explain the invariant
$G\bm 1_K=\bm 0_N$, which is the differential counterpart of row-wise shift
invariance.

        \subsection{Theory--engineering gap inventory}

        \begin{gapbox}
        \textbf{Explicit gap.} A correct symbolic derivative still leaves batch reduction, stable probability evaluation, sparse indexing, dtype, and output-shape decisions.

        \textbf{Implicit gap exposed by the result.} Row-sum zero can pass while coordinates are scaled by the wrong batch factor; finite differences and the invariant are therefore complementary evidence.
        \end{gapbox}

        \subsection{Acceptance evidence}

        \begin{evidencebox}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item All row sums are near zero and the output shape/dtype match the logits.
  \item Every coordinate of a small fixture agrees with a central-difference derivative of the stable scalar loss.
  \item The benchmark prints maximum absolute error, maximum relative error, and shift-null residual separately. (Trust-based: the test suite does not machine-check benchmark output.)
        \end{itemize}
        \end{evidencebox}

        \subsection{Constraints}

        \begin{itemize}
          \item No PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX, autograd, or finite differences inside the learner function.
  \item Support finite rank-two float64 logits and one-dimensional integer labels in $[0,K)$ paired with the batch; reject out-of-domain inputs with \code{ValueError}.
  \item Use the mean reduction exactly as stated.
        \end{itemize}
