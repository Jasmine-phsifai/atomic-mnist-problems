        \subsection{Mathematical statement}


For observations $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, Welford's recurrence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_n&=x_n-\mu_{n-1},\\
\mu_n&=\mu_{n-1}+\delta_n/n,\\
M_{2,n}&=M_{2,n-1}+\delta_n(x_n-\mu_n),\\
s^2&=M_{2,n}/(n-\mathrm{ddof}).
\end{aligned}
\]
It avoids subtracting two numbers of order $\mu^2$ to recover a small variance.

        \subsection{Theory--engineering gap inventory}

        \begin{gapbox}
        \textbf{Explicit gap.} The identity $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2$ is exact over reals but can subtract nearly equal rounded quantities in software.

        \textbf{Implicit gap exposed by the result.} Adding a $10^{12}$ offset leaves mathematical variance unchanged yet can destroy the naive one-pass formula; Welford and a centered oracle remain stable.
        \end{gapbox}

        \subsection{Acceptance evidence}

        \begin{evidencebox}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Mean and variance match NumPy's two-pass oracle on large-offset data for both allowed \code{ddof} values.
  \item Constant arrays return exactly zero variance rather than a small negative value.
  \item The benchmark prints naive/Welford errors and offset-invariance error separately.
        \end{itemize}
        \end{evidencebox}
