\problemstart{AP-01-008}{Epoch Means Are Sample-Weighted}{Difficulty 2/5}{Chapter 1 \textbullet\ numpy}{Aggregate unequal batch means without giving a short tail batch the weight of a full batch.}

        \subsection{Mathematical statement}


If batch $j$ has $n_j$ samples and reported mean $m_j$, the epoch mean is
\[
  \bar m=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{B}n_jm_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{B}n_j}.
\]
The commonly written expression $B^{-1}\sum_jm_j$ equals $\bar m$ only under
special conditions, such as equal batch sizes.  Define the aggregation bias
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{batch}}=\frac1B\sum_jm_j-\bar m.
\]

        \subsection{Theory--engineering gap inventory}

        \begin{gapbox}
        \textbf{Explicit gap.} A mean over samples is not the same measure as a mean over batches; the notation must identify which finite set carries uniform weight.

        \textbf{Implicit gap exposed by the result.} A two-sample tail with a deliberately high loss makes the naive estimator's bias visible while all individual batch means remain valid.
        \end{gapbox}

        \subsection{Acceptance evidence}

        \begin{evidencebox}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item The adversarial $(64,64,2)$ fixture matches the direct per-sample oracle.
  \item The printed \(\Delta_{\mathrm{batch}}\) is nonzero, while the implementation error is near floating-point roundoff.
  \item Invalid sizes, NaN means, and shape mismatches fail explicitly.
        \end{itemize}
        \end{evidencebox}

        \subsection{Constraints}

        \begin{itemize}
          \item Do not reconstruct per-sample arrays in the implementation.
  \item Use float64 accumulation.
  \item Do not silently drop zero-sized batches; reject them.
        \end{itemize}
