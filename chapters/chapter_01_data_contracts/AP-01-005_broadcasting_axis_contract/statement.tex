\problemstart{AP-01-005}{Broadcasting With a Named Feature Axis}{Difficulty 3/5}{Chapter 1 \textbullet\ numpy}{Standardize a batch while making the intended feature axis explicit and rejecting accidental broadcasts.}

        \subsection{Mathematical statement}


For $X\in\mathbb R^{N\times D}$ and feature statistics
$\bm\mu,\bm s\in\mathbb R^D$ with $s_j>0$, compute
\[
  Z_{ij}=\frac{X_{ij}-\mu_j}{s_j},
  \qquad
  Z=(X-\bm 1_N\bm\mu^\top)\operatorname{diag}(\bm s)^{-1}.
\]
The named feature alignment is
\[
\bm 1_N\bm\mu^\top=
\left[\begin{matrix}
\mu_1 & \cdots & \mu_D\\
\vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\
\mu_1 & \cdots & \mu_D
\end{matrix}\right],
\]
not a column of per-example statistics.
A scalar pair is also admissible.  No other statistic shape belongs to the
domain, even if NumPy could broadcast it in some accidental configuration.

        \subsection{Theory--engineering gap inventory}

        \begin{gapbox}
        \textbf{Explicit gap.} Broadcast compatibility is a syntactic shape relation; it does not prove that the aligned axis has the intended statistical meaning.

        \textbf{Implicit gap exposed by the result.} A square $N=D$ fixture makes row-wise and feature-wise vectors equally broadcastable, so only the specified value oracle distinguishes them.
        \end{gapbox}

        \subsection{Acceptance evidence}

        \begin{evidencebox}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item The square ambiguity fixture matches the feature-axis matrix equation exactly.
  \item Scalar statistics work; shapes $(N,1)$ and other accidental broadcasts fail.
  \item The benchmark prints per-feature residual means and an axis-confusion distance.
        \end{itemize}
        \end{evidencebox}

        \subsection{Constraints}

        \begin{itemize}
          \item Use vectorized NumPy broadcasting.
  \item Do not infer an axis from equal dimension sizes.
  \item Reject zero, negative, NaN, or infinite scale entries.
        \end{itemize}
